2. Combine 200 g white cane sugar and 200 g water in a small saucepan. Heat gently, stirring until sugar dissolves; approximately 80–85°C if using a thermometer. Avoid prolonged boiling so the concentration stays close to 1:1 by weight.
3. Remove from heat, add the 6 g rosemary, cover, and steep 10 minutes. Record actual infusion start time when reported.
4. Taste a cooled spoonful diluted with a little water. If rosemary is too faint, steep 5 more minutes; otherwise strain out rosemary at 10 minutes. Record actual total steep.
5. Cool the strained syrup in an ice-water bath, keeping bath water out. Transfer to a clean sealed pouring container and refrigerate. Weigh finished syrup in a tared container and record the actual yield.

## Method

1. Combine coffee and brew water, stir thoroughly, cover, and refrigerate for 18–24 hours. Measure container fit without filling to the brim; an internal filter may reduce usable capacity.
2. Strain and paper-filter. Weigh actual filtered concentrate. Estimated small-batch yield was 350–400 g, not guaranteed.
3. Reserve 60 g concentrate for up to three 20 g test portions. Record actual test consumption before scaling service batch.
4. Dissolve syrup sugar in water with gentle heat, approximately 85°C; remove from heat. Add rosemary and cover. Taste a cooled spoonful after 10 minutes; strain when the aroma is suitable. Record actual steep time. Cool and refrigerate separately.
5. Keep concentrate, syrup, lime, and dilution water separate until the ratio is selected.
6. After testing, premeasure only the required service syrup. Coffee can be combined with its measured dilution water for transport.
7. Tuesday: squeeze and strain fresh lime; tare the syrup bottle on the scale and add the measured juice. Mix, then pour all into the coffee pitcher and stir thoroughly. Rinse residual syrup into the pitcher with a small amount of the already measured coffee mixture.
8. Prefer one premixed service batch. Keep ice separate and add to cups just before pouring.
